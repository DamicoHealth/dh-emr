DH EMR Field guides

User guide

The day-to-day reference for everyone who enters paper charts into DH EMR Field: the visit form section by section, saving, finding patients, returning patients, deleting, analytics, the sync chip, the warning banners, and updates. Setting up a device is in Getting started. Changing the clinic's lists and forms is admin work and lives in the admin guide.

Two words the app uses everywhere: a visit is one patient seen on one day, and the patient number is the short code the app creates for each patient. The app makes the patient number by itself from the name and date of birth. You never type it.

1. The screens

Field has four tabs. On a phone they sit at the bottom of the screen; on a tablet or computer they are in the top bar next to the name "DH EMR Field".

  • Visits: every visit saved on this device, with search and filters. This is where you find a patient. It is the tab the app opens on.
  • New visit: opens the visit form on top of whatever screen you are on.
  • Analytics: counts and charts built from the visits on the device.
  • Settings: device name, cloud sync, backups, and the clinic's lists and forms. Most of it is for admins.

At the right of the top bar is a small chip that tells you whether records are backed up to your cloud. It is explained in section 10. There is no sign-in and no account: the device itself is the identity.

2. Entering a visit

Tap New visit in the tab bar, or + New visit on the Visits screen. The visit form slides in as a panel. On a phone it fills the screen; on a wider screen it opens on the right. Tapping outside the panel does not close it, on purpose. Only Cancel closes it, and if you have typed anything the app asks first: "Discard this visit? Anything you have entered will be lost." Tap OK to discard, or Cancel to keep working.

At the top of the panel is the title, for example "New visit", and under it the patient number. It first says "Patient number generates from name + date of birth" and changes to "Patient number" followed by the number once you have entered both, with the age after it.

The form is a list of sections. Five of them start closed: Procedures, Referral, Imaging, Surgery and Notes. A closed section shows a "+" on its header; tap the header to open it. Your clinic may have renamed, hidden or reordered sections, and may have added its own, so the order below is the default. Required fields show a red "*" after the label.

If your clinic offers more than one form, a Form template list appears at the top of a new visit so you can pick the right one. Every visit remembers which form it was filed under.

Visit

Site (required), Date (required), and Provider. The date starts as today. When you enter a paper chart, change it to the date on the chart. The app refuses a date in the future: "Visit date is in the future." The site you pick is remembered for the next patient.

Patient

  • Given name and Family name, both required. The patient number is built from these plus the date of birth, so spell them the same way every time the patient comes back. Both need at least two letters. Names in any script work.
  • Sex, required: tap M or F. Tap again to clear it.
  • Date of birth, typed as DD/MM/YYYY. If the patient does not know it, tick Estimate from age next to "Date of birth unknown" and enter an Estimated age in years instead. A date of birth in the future is refused.
  • Phone, optional.
  • Patient number, read only, with the hint "generated automatically". The app fills it in.

Vitals

Temperature is in °C. If you enter a number outside 25 to 45, the save is refused and the message asks whether it was entered in Fahrenheit. Blood pressure is written like 120/80. Weight is in kg. When Sex is not M, two more questions appear: Pregnant and Breastfeeding, each answered with Yes or No. Choosing M clears both.

History

Allergies, Current medications and Past medical history. For a returning patient these can be carried forward instead of retyped; see section 7.

Chief Concern

Type the concern in the Chief concern box, or tap the complaint buttons under it, for example "Fever", "Cough" or "Abdominal Pain". Tapping a button adds that complaint to the text; tapping it again removes just that entry. You can mix buttons and your own typing.

Labs

One tile per test your clinic offers. A positive/negative test has POS and NEG buttons. A number test, for example "Blood Glucose" or "Hemoglobin", has a number box with the unit shown inside it, and the app records a label with the value, such as "Normal" or "High", using the ranges your admin set. There is a Lab comments box, and a Urinalysis block with one row of buttons per parameter (leukocytes, nitrite, protein and so on). Only record tests that were actually done; empty tiles are simply left empty.

Diagnosis

Type in the Diagnosis box, or tap the quick-pick buttons under it, for example "Malaria" or "URTI". They work like the complaint buttons: tap to add, tap again to remove that entry.

Medications

Each medication is one line: pick the drug from the list (its first entry reads "Select medication"), type the dose (for example "500mg" or "2 tabs"), then pick a frequency and a duration. Picking a drug fills the dose box from the formulary if the box is still empty. Tap + Add medication for another line and the × at the end of a line to remove it. There is a Treatment notes box at the bottom of the section.

If your clinic has set up prescription presets, a row headed Prescription presets appears at the top of the section, with one button per preset, for example "UTI" or "Malaria >35kg". Tapping one adds its medication lines and its treatment notes in one go. It only ever adds; it never removes lines, and you can edit or delete the added lines like any others.

Next to each line, once a drug is chosen, the app shows the quantity to hand out:

Next to the lineWhat it means
"20 tabs"The dose was understood. This is the quantity to dispense for the chosen frequency and duration.
"20 tabs?"The dose could not be read, so the app assumed 1 per dose. Check it. Writing the dose as a number of tabs or in mg fixes the guess.
"ongoing"The duration is Ongoing, so no quantity is calculated.
"no qty"The dose could not be read at all. No quantity will be recorded for this line.

Procedures, Referral, Imaging, Surgery, Notes

  • Procedures: tap the buttons for what was done, for example "Wound Closure/Sutures".
  • Referral: pick a Referral type. The first entry is "None". Once a type is chosen, a Scheduled date box appears.
  • Imaging: Ultrasound type and Findings.
  • Surgery: tick Yes next to "Surgery performed" to open the Type and Notes boxes.
  • Notes: a free text box for anything else.

Your clinic's own sections

An admin can add sections with their own questions: short and long text, a single choice from a list, several choices with checkboxes, numbers, a slider, Yes / No buttons, and dates. They work like the built-in fields. A required question is marked with "*" the same way, and if you leave one blank the save is refused with the section and question named, for example "Social History: Smokes is required."

3. Saving a visit

At the bottom of the form there are two save buttons. Both read "Saving…" while the visit is written.

  • Save files the visit and closes the panel.
  • Save & next patient files the visit and keeps the form open for the next chart. Site, Date and Provider stay filled in; everything else clears. A green note confirms the save for a few seconds, for example "Saved Grace Auma (GRAU03021988). Ready for the next patient." This button only appears on new visits, never while editing an old one.

If something is missing or wrong, the visit is not saved. A red summary appears, for example "Check 2 fields", listing each problem in plain words ("Family name is required.", "Site is required.", "Visit date is in the future.") and the form jumps to the first field that needs attention. Fix the fields and save again.

Very rarely the device itself refuses to save, and the form shows a red "Not saved" notice with the reason. One reason reads: "Your existing records could not be read from this device right now, so saving was blocked to avoid overwriting them. Please close and reopen the app; if it keeps happening, restart the device before entering more data." Do what it says. The other reason says the device may be out of space or in a private-browsing window, and that your entry was not saved. Nothing already saved is touched in either case.

4. If the app closes mid-visit

While you type, the form keeps a working copy on the device. If the app reloads or gets pushed out of memory before you save, because of a phone call, opening the camera, or the screen locking for a long time, your typing is not gone. The next time the same visit form opens, a blue notice appears: "Recovered from where you left off." It says who the draft is for and the time you last typed, for example "Recovered for Amara Nakato, last typed at" followed by the time, and ends "Check every field before saving, then save as usual." Do exactly that.

The recovered copy only comes back on the visit it belongs to. It is never offered on a different patient's form, it does not survive closing the app completely, and it is cleared the moment you save or discard.

Do not lean on this net. It covers interruptions, not everything. Save each visit as soon as it is complete.

5. Finding a patient

Open the Visits tab. Across the top are four counts for the whole device: Visits, Patients, Today and Pediatric (<18). They always cover everything on the device, even while you search.

Type in the search box (its hint reads "Search name, patient number, concern, diagnosis...") to find someone by name, patient number, concern, diagnosis, site or provider. Tap Filters to narrow the list by Site, Provider, Referral, or a date range with From and To; Reset clears them. When filters are active the button shows how many, for example "Filters (2)".

Each patient is one card, most recently seen first: the name, then the latest site, the latest date, sex and age, and "No." followed by the patient number, then the latest diagnosis. Badges flag a positive lab ("Lab+"), a referral ("Referred"), pregnancy ("Pregnant"), and how many visits the patient has, for example "3 visits".

Three messages can replace the list. "No visits recorded on this device yet." means the device is empty. "No visits match those filters." means your search or filters excluded everything; tap Reset. On a device with more than 150 patients the list shows the first 150 and says so; type a name to narrow it.

6. The patient chart

Tap a card to open the patient's chart, a panel over the list. Here tapping outside the panel does close it, because there is nothing typed to lose. The header shows the name, then "Patient number" with the number, sex and age, and "DOB" with the date of birth when known ("(est.)" after it when the age was estimated). There are two buttons: + New visit to start a new visit for this patient, and Close.

The body starts with the visit count. Once a patient has two or more visits, a Vitals trend card appears: small graphs for temperature and weight, and a table of Temp, BP and Weight across the visits, oldest first as V1, V2 and so on, with the date under each.

Below that, every visit is its own card, newest first: "Visit 3" and so on, then the date, site and provider, and the name of the form it was filed under. Each visit card has an Edit button and a Delete button, then blocks for Vitals, History, Labs (positive results in red), Urinalysis, Clinical (diagnosis, each medication line with its quantity, treatment notes, procedures, imaging, surgery, referral, notes) and Additional (your clinic's own questions). Empty rows are not shown.

7. A returning patient

When a patient comes back, do not edit their old visit. Start a new one. The easiest way: find them on the Visits tab, open their chart, and tap + New visit. The form opens headed "New visit for" their name, with their identity already filled in: name, sex, date of birth, phone, patient number. Today's vitals, labs, diagnosis and medications start blank, because today's findings must be entered for today.

If you instead start a blank visit and type a name and date of birth the app already knows, a blue Returning patient notice appears. It shows how many previous visits there are, when the patient was last seen, and the last diagnosis, with one button: Copy allergies, history and medications forward. Tap it to pull the allergies, past medical history, current medications and phone from the last visit instead of retyping them. It fills only boxes you have left empty.

Editing an old visit

Edit on a visit card opens that visit, headed "Edit visit", and Save overwrites it. Use it to fix a mistake in that day's record: a wrong weight, a missed lab result. A new visit records a new day. If you edit last month's visit to document today, last month's record is gone, and the patient's visit count and history become wrong. When in doubt: fixing the past is Edit, documenting today is a new visit.

On a cloud device, two warnings can appear when you save an edit, because another device may have touched the same visit while you had it open:

If the visit was deleted elsewhere

"This visit was DELETED on another device while you had it open.

Saving now brings it back for everyone. Cancel instead if the deletion was correct."

If the visit was changed elsewhere

"This visit was changed on another device while you had it open.

Saving now replaces those changes with what is on your screen. Cancel instead to close without saving and reopen the visit to see them first."

Read the message and choose. Cancel never loses anything that is already saved.

8. Deleting a visit

Open the patient's chart and tap Delete on the visit card. The app asks first, and the warning means exactly what it says:

The delete confirm

"Delete the 12/04/2026 visit for Amara Nakato?

The visit is removed from this device and from every device that syncs with it. This cannot be undone from inside the app - only a backup taken before now can bring it back."

After you tap OK, the Visits tab shows a bar: "Deleted the 12/04/2026 visit for Amara Nakato." with a Dismiss button. A deleted visit does not come back on the next sync; on a cloud device the deletion itself syncs, so the visit disappears from every device and stays gone. The only way back is restoring a backup file made before the deletion. So before tapping OK, be sure you have the right visit for the right patient.

9. Analytics

The Analytics tab is built from the visits stored on the device you are holding. On a cloud device that includes the whole team's visits once sync has run. Filter by date range (From and To), Site (first entry "All sites") and Provider (first entry "All providers"); Reset clears everything. On an empty device it says "No visits recorded on this device yet. Charts appear here once visits are saved."

The tiles across the top:

  • Visits: visits matching the filters.
  • Patients: how many different patients those visits belong to.
  • New patients: patients seen for the first time, with the number of return visits underneath.
  • Referrals: visits with a referral recorded.
  • Lab positive: visits with at least one positive test.
  • Median age: the middle age across those visits, in years. It shows "-" when no visit has a date of birth or estimated age.

The panels below:

  • Visits over time: daily visits and unique patients. Tap View data under a chart to see the numbers as a table.
  • Age and sex: who you are seeing, split female and male by age band.
  • Lab positivity: for each test, what share of the tests ordered came back positive, for example "32% (8 of 25 tested)".
  • Top diagnoses and Top medications: the most recorded diagnoses and the most dispensed drugs.
  • Visits by site and Visits by provider: where the work happened and who did it.
  • Data quality: the share of visits with no diagnosis, no vitals, or no provider recorded. A share above 25% is highlighted. Check this before a donor report, and tighten up entry where a number is high.

10. The sync chip

The chip at the right of the top bar tells you whether records are backed up to your organization's cloud. On a device set up on its own it reads Offline only and cannot be tapped. On a cloud device, tapping it runs a sync by hand, and the label reads "Syncing…" while it runs. A small badge like "3 pending" means records are waiting to upload. The badge is not shown on a phone-sized screen; there, the Waiting to sync row on the Cloud sync card in Settings always shows the count.

Chip saysWhat it meansWhat to do
Offline onlyThis device does not use cloud sync. Records stay on the device.Take a backup at the end of every day of entry: Settings, then Download backup.
ConnectedThe cloud is set up but no sync has run yet this session.Tap the chip to sync.
OfflineNo internet right now. Everything still works; records save to the device.Nothing. It syncs when the connection returns.
SyncingRecords are being sent and received.Wait a moment.
SyncedAll records are backed up to your cloud.Nothing.
Sync errorThe last sync failed.Tap the chip to retry and check the connection. If it stays red, tell your admin.

If you sync by hand and records still cannot upload, the app says so plainly: "Not backed up." followed by how many records are still waiting and why, for example "This device is offline." or "The cloud rejected the upload or could not be reached." It ends: "Try again when you have signal. Do not wipe or hand on this device until this clears." The waiting records exist only on that device, so until the message stops appearing, do not wipe the device, hand it to someone else, or delete the app.

11. Warning banners and what to do

Sometimes a colored banner appears at the top of the app. Each one means something specific, and each one has a right response.

Amber: "Back this device up today."

The device is down to a single copy of its records: the app's own on-device safety copy has never been written or is out of date. The records you can see are fine, but there is no second copy behind them. The banner itself says what to do: "Download a backup now and at the end of every clinic day, and ask your admin to archive older visits off this device." So:

  1. Go to Settings and tap Download backup now. Keep the file somewhere safe, off the device.
  2. Tell your admin.

Red: "This device cannot read its records. Do not enter more patients here."

Stop entering patients on this device and use another device for today. Your records are not gone. The banner appears because the app found an older on-device copy, decided it is out of date, and refused to load it so nothing overwrites anything. Saving is blocked while the banner shows. Then:

  1. If you have a backup file anywhere, tap Go to Settings to restore a backup and restore it. This is the safest way back.
  2. Only if there is no backup file at all, tap Use the older copy instead. The app asks first and tells you how many records the older copy holds, how many this device last had, and that anything entered after that copy was made will not come back. Read it carefully before tapping OK. If it works, the app says "Loaded 140 records from the older copy. Take a backup now, before entering anything else." Do that.
  3. Tell your admin either way.

Red: "Close the other tabs."

The app is open in more than one browser tab or window on this device, and this browser cannot keep them in step. Close the extra tabs right away. Records entered in one tab can overwrite the other.

"Something went wrong displaying this screen."

A screen failed to draw. The message says: "Your records are still on this device. Nothing has been deleted, and anything you had already saved is safe." Tap Try again. If it happens again, close the app completely and reopen it. If it still happens, take a backup from Settings on a working device before doing anything else.

"The app could not start"

The device could not open its records store at all. The screen says "Your records have not been changed or deleted." and lists three steps: close the app completely and open it again; if that fails, restart the device; if it still will not start, use a different device for today and tell your admin. There is a Reload button.

Whatever goes wrong, never "fix" it by clearing the browser's data or deleting and reinstalling the app. That erases any records that have not synced or been backed up, and nothing can bring them back. Every error screen in the app repeats this warning for a reason.

12. Updating the app

The app never updates itself. An update reloads the app, and a reload in the middle of a visit is exactly the interruption you do not need, so updating is always your choice, at your moment.

When a new version is ready, a bar appears: "A new version of DH EMR is ready." with the note "Updating reloads the app, so finish and save any open visit first. Saved records are not affected." Finish the visit, then tap Update now. Not now hides the bar until the next time the app starts, when it offers again.

Take updates when they are offered, between charts or at the end of the day. Fixes only reach the device when someone taps Update now.
