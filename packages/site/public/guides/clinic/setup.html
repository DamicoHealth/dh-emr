DH EMR Clinic guides

Setting up DH EMR Clinic for your organization

For the person who will run the organization's records: the organization lead, who becomes the first administrator. This guide goes from nothing to a live clinic. You create a cloud project that your organization owns, set up your own device, make yourself the first administrator, invite the staff devices with a link or a QR code, approve each staff account and give it a role, and watch the board come alive. Most of it is done once. Plan an hour the first time, plus a few minutes per staff device. No command line is needed.

1. What you are setting up

DH EMR Clinic is one app that every device in the clinic opens in its browser, at damicohealth.com/clinic/. Behind it sits one cloud project that your organization creates and owns. Damico Health hosts nothing and sees nothing: there is no central server, no account with us, and no subscription. Your records live on your devices and in your project.

The pieces, in the order you will build them:

  • The project. A Supabase project, set up from its dashboard in a browser. It holds the records, the staff accounts and the organization's settings.
  • Your own device. The one device where the project address and key are typed by hand. Every other device gets them inside a join link.
  • The first administrator: you. Administrators see a Staff tab, approve accounts and set roles, and are the only accounts that can change the organization's lists, formulary, lab tests, presets and form templates. The first one is made from the project dashboard, once. After that, administrators make other administrators from the app.
  • The staff devices. Each one opens the join link from your Staff screen, or scans its QR code, and the app sets itself up and shows sign-in. Nobody types a project address or key.
  • The staff accounts. Each person creates their own account on their device. A new account can see nothing until you approve it and give it a role: Reception, Triage, Provider, Lab or Pharmacy.
  • The board. Patient flow shows today's visits as cards moving across stations. Every device sees a change within seconds.

Admin means the account, not the device. In Clinic there is one kind of admin: the Admin flag on a staff account. It unlocks the Staff tab, it unlocks the organization's settings editors (the clinic lists, formulary, lab tests, presets and form templates) under Settings on whichever device that account is signed in on, and the server enforces it. Devices have no admin role in Clinic: a device that joined by link is as good as any other, and what a person can change follows the account they sign in with. On a device where a non-administrator is signed in, the editors are visible but turned off, with the note "Only an administrator account can change this. Ask your clinic's admin."

2. What you need

  • Internet all day, for every device. Clinic is built for a constant connection: the board, the Lab and Pharmacy screens and staff sign-in all depend on it. Section 14 says what happens when it goes down.
  • A Supabase account, at supabase.com. The free tier is enough for a small clinic.
  • One administrator to start: you. An email address and a password you will remember. The app has no password reset of its own, so write the password down somewhere safe.
  • A computer with a browser for the project dashboard. It is easier on a large screen.
  • The two SQL files that set up and check the project: setup.sql and verify.sql. They live in the supabase folder of the DH EMR source code, next to the full step-by-step in SETUP.md. Until they are published on this site, that is where they live.
  • Your own device: a tablet, a laptop or a phone with a modern browser. On most tablets and phones the browser can add the app to the home screen so it opens like an app.
  • About an hour, plus a few minutes per staff device.

3. Create the project

  1. On supabase.com, choose New project. Any name. Pick the region closest to the clinic.
  2. Generate a strong database password and store it in a password manager. It is rarely needed again.
  3. Wait for the project to finish provisioning.

4. Run the schema

  1. In the left sidebar, open SQL Editor, then New query.
  2. Paste the entire contents of setup.sql and click Run.
  3. It is safe to run twice; re-running is the documented upgrade path.
  4. Recommended: paste and run verify.sql next. Every row of its output must say PASS. It changes nothing; it ends by rolling itself back.

The schema creates the tables, the security rules that make every read and write need an approved account, and the server-side checks behind the Staff screen. It also turns on live updates for the records and settings tables; that is what lets every device see a change within seconds.

If you are upgrading a project that is already in use, run it after clinic hours. The run briefly locks the records table, and a device syncing during those seconds shows a sync error, retries, and succeeds afterward. Nothing is lost either way; it is just alarming to watch mid-clinic.

5. Get the app credentials

  1. Project URL: Project Settings, then Data API. It is also shown in the Connect dialog at the top of the dashboard. It looks like https://yourproject.supabase.co.
  2. Publishable key (sb_publishable_...): Project Settings, then API Keys.

You will type these two values exactly once, on your own device in section 8. Every other device gets them inside the join link in section 9.

The key rule. Devices get the publishable key, or the legacy anon key, and nothing else. Never put the sb_secret_... key or a legacy service_role key on any device, in any chat, or in the app. It bypasses every security rule. The app refuses keys that look like it, on purpose, with the message: "That is a server key (service_role or sb_secret). It bypasses every database security rule and must never go on a field device. Use the Publishable key, or the legacy anon key."

6. Turn on sign-in

Clinic uses staff accounts, so the project must allow email sign-in. In the dashboard:

  1. Authentication, then Sign In / Up: enable the Email provider.
  2. Turn off "Confirm email".

The second step matters more than it looks. Without your own mail server configured, the project's built-in mailer is restricted and heavily rate limited, so confirmation emails to clinic staff mostly never arrive and accounts strand half-created. Approval inside the app replaces what confirmation was for: a new account starts with no access at all, and only an administrator can open it. For the same reason, do not rely on email password resets; section 15 is the way back in.

7. Create the first administrator

There is no self-service way to become the first administrator, on purpose: anyone in the world who obtains the project address and publishable key can create an account, so creating an account must carry no power. The first administrator is made from the dashboard, once.

  1. Create your account: Authentication, then Users, then Add user. Choose "Create new user" and set a password. Do not use "Send invitation": it depends on the same restricted mailer.
  2. Make that account the administrator. Back in the SQL Editor, in a new query, run this one statement with your own email in place of LEAD@EXAMPLE.ORG:
UPDATE users_profiles
SET is_admin = true, activated_at = now()
WHERE id = (SELECT id FROM auth.users WHERE lower(email) = lower('LEAD@EXAMPLE.ORG'))
RETURNING id, is_admin, activated_at;

The editor must show exactly one returned row. Zero rows means the email did not match or the account does not exist yet; check the spelling and run it again. That one row is the whole bootstrap: it approves the account and flags it as an administrator at the same time.

Another way to make the account. You can also create your account from the app itself, on the sign-in screen in section 8, with New here? Create an account. The app then shows "Waiting for approval". Run the same statement above, then tap Check again. Either way, the statement is what makes the account an administrator.

There is no mode switch step. Older setup notes had a manual step to set the organization to clinic mode by hand. With DH EMR Clinic that is automatic: the first time an administrator signs in on a Clinic device, the app sets it. There is nothing to run and nothing to tap. The SQL for doing it by hand is still in SETUP.md as a fallback; you do not need it.

8. Set up your own device

Open damicohealth.com/clinic/ on the device you will use. The first time, the app shows a short wizard. For Clinic it opens straight on the cloud step. There is no choice to use the device on its own, because a Clinic device always belongs to a project.

Step one: Connect to your cloud

The step is headed Connect to your cloud. A note at the top says "Open the link your admin sent you", because that is how every other device will arrive. You are the admin and there is no link yet, so you type instead. The note says so: "No link? Your admin can read you the project address and key instead."

  1. In Project URL, paste the Project URL from section 5.
  2. In Project key, paste the publishable key.
  3. Tap Check and continue. The button reads "Checking…" while the app contacts the project and checks that its tables exist.

Step two: Name this device

The step is headed Name this device. In Device name, type something the team will recognise; the wizard suggests the form "iPad 2 - triage" or "Grace's tablet". The name shows up in the device list and on the records this device files.

The step also asks What is this device allowed to do?, with Enter and view records (most devices) and Also change the form and clinic settings for everyone. In Clinic this choice changes nothing: what a person can change under Settings follows the account they sign in with, not the device, so leave it on the first option. (The same wizard serves DH EMR Field, where the choice does matter.)

Tap Next.

Step three: Where is this device working?

The step is headed Where is this device working?. Fill Clinic or site names and Clinicians working on this device, one entry per line. These fill the Site and Provider lists on the visit form. Because you will sign in as an administrator on this device, these two lists go out to the organization on the first sync and reach every staff device, so enter the real site names and the real clinicians now. You can change them later under Settings.

Both lists need at least one entry. The wizard refuses an empty one: "You need at least one site. An empty list would block every save." and the same for clinicians. Commas belong inside an entry; "Grace N., clinical officer" is one clinician.

Tap Start using the app. The button reads "Finishing…" while the device registers itself in the project. If the project refuses at this moment, the wizard says "Could not finish setup:" with the reason. Nothing was stored; try again.

Then: Sign in

The wizard is followed by a screen headed Sign in: "This organization uses staff accounts. Sign in with the email and password for your account." Enter the Email and Password you set in section 7 and tap Sign in. The button reads "Signing in…" while the project checks the account.

What happens next, in order:

  • The app lands on the Board tab, headed Patient flow, with the card "No patients on the board yet". Your tabs are Board, Visits, New visit, Analytics, Staff and Settings. Only administrators see Staff.
  • Because this is the first administrator sign-in, the app sets the organization to clinic mode by itself. Nothing shows on screen for it.
  • The chip in the top bar runs the first sync. Your site and clinician lists go out to the organization. Within a moment the chip reads Live.

Check it: open Settings. The Account card shows your Name, your Role (a new account starts as Provider; you can change it in section 11) and Admin Yes. The This device card shows the device's Name, Device ID and Mode; there is no device role in Clinic. Further down, the Clinic lists, Formulary and Lab tests cards are editable, because you are an administrator.

If instead of the Board you see a screen headed Waiting for approval, the statement in section 7 did not return a row. Run it again, check for one returned row, then tap Check again.

9. Invite the staff devices

Open the Staff tab. The first card is Invite a device. Its own words: "Staff open this link on their device, or scan the code with its camera. The app sets itself up and shows sign-in; they create an account there, and you approve it below. Nobody types a project address or key."

  1. Fill Organization name, for example the clinic's name. The hint says why to do it first: "Shown on a device when it joins. It travels in the link, so set it before you share." The link regenerates as you type.
  2. The Join link box holds the link. Tap Copy link; the card says "Link copied. Paste it into a message to your staff." If the device cannot copy, it says "Could not copy automatically. Select the link above and copy it by hand."
  3. Below the link is the QR code of the same link. A device in the room can scan it instead of receiving a message.

The link is the same for every device and does not expire. Keep it in a message thread the staff can find. If you change the organization name later, copy the link again, because the name travels inside it. A very long organization name can make the link too long for a QR code; the card then says "The link is too long for a QR code. Share the link itself instead."

Who gets the link. The card's footer says exactly what it carries: "What the link carries: the project address, its publishable key and the organization name. The publishable key is public by design and unlocks nothing on its own, because every read and write needs an approved account. Anyone with the link can create an account that waits for your approval, so share it with staff, not the public."

On each staff device

Open the link in the device's browser, or point the camera app at the QR code and open the link it offers. The app configures the device before any wizard can appear and lands on the Sign in screen. That is all; there is nothing to type and nothing to choose. The device registers itself in the project, named after its platform and the date, for example "iPad joined 2026-10-06". Rename it later under Settings, on the This device card, with Rename this device. Renaming is always safe: it changes the label only.

A joined device does not ask for sites and clinicians. The organization's lists arrive on its first sync, after its account is approved.

If the link cannot be used, the reason shows on the device, never a silent fallback: on the wizard as a red alert, or on the sign-in screen as a banner that starts "That join link could not be used."

The device saysWhat to do
"That join link is damaged or incomplete. Ask your admin to send it again."The link was cut short in the message. Copy it again from the Invite card and send the whole thing.
"That join link was made by a newer version of DH EMR Clinic. Update this app, then open the link again."The device has an old version of the app open. Reload it, accept the update if the app offers one, then open the link again.
"Could not join" with the organization name and a reason, or "the project could not be verified."The project refused the device. Most often the schema in section 4 has not been run.
"Could not reach" with the organization name, then "Check the device is online and open the link again."No signal. Connect the device and open the link again.

Opening the link on a device that is already set up for this project does nothing, which is fine. Opening it on a device that is set up for a different project asks first: "This device is already connected to" the old project, then "Switch it to" the new one, with the explanation that visits already synced to the old project stay there and anything not yet backed up uploads to the new one. Cancel unless that switch is what you want.

A device that cannot open the link

If a device cannot receive the link and has no camera, read it the Project URL and the publishable key from section 5. On that device the wizard's Connect to your cloud step takes them, exactly as yours did in section 8. Leave What is this device allowed to do? on its first option; on the sites step, type one site and one clinician to satisfy the wizard. The organization's real lists replace them on the first sync.

Which device can change the organization's settings

Any device, as long as an administrator account is signed in on it. There is no admin device to set up, nothing to run in the dashboard for it, and a device that joined by link is no different from your own. Sign in as an administrator on a joined device and its Settings editors open; sign in as a non-administrator on your own device and they are turned off with the note "Only an administrator account can change this. Ask your clinic's admin." The admin guide explains the gate; its Devices section covers the device list, renaming and revoking.

10. Staff create their accounts

On the Sign in screen, each staff member taps New here? Create an account. The screen changes to Create your account and explains what will happen: "A new account starts with no access at all. Your administrator approves it from their device, and then you can sign in and work. That usually takes a moment, not a day."

  1. Your name: the name the team knows them by. It shows on the Staff list and on the records they save.
  2. Email and Password.
  3. Tap Create account. The button reads "Creating…" while the project makes the account.

The device then shows a screen headed Waiting for approval. It names their account and says it "is set up and waiting for your administrator to approve it. That is a normal step for every new account, and it usually takes them only a moment." It tells them what to do: "Ask whoever runs this organization's records to approve you, then tap Check again." The screen has two buttons, Check again and Sign out. A waiting account sees no records at all.

If the device has no signal at that moment, the heading is Cannot confirm your account yet instead, and the text says the device could not reach the organization's server. Get signal and tap Check again.

If, after Create account, the screen goes back to Sign in with the notice "Your account was created. Sign in with the same email and password once your administrator has approved it.", the "Confirm email" setting from section 6 was left on. Turn it off in the dashboard. The account still exists; approve it as usual, and the person signs in afterwards.

A person who forgets their password cannot reset it from the app. The sign-in screen says so: "Forgot your password? Your administrator can set up a fresh account for you - password reset emails are not reliable on most clinic projects." They create a new account with a different email, and you approve it.

11. Approve accounts and set roles

On your device, open the Staff tab and find the Staff accounts card. Its own words: "Approve new accounts, set station roles, and revoke access. A new account can see nothing until it is approved here. Changes are enforced by the server: a device that is offline keeps its last confirmed access and is cut off the moment it reconnects."

Before anyone has joined, the card says "No one else is here yet." and repeats the invite steps. Once accounts arrive, a note counts them: "1 account is waiting for your approval." or the number. Each account is a row with the person's name, a status chip that reads Pending, Active or Revoked, a chip with the role, and an Admin chip on administrators. Your own row says "(you)". Oldest accounts are listed first.

For each new account:

  1. Set the role first. The role select on the row offers Reception, Triage, Provider, Lab and Pharmacy. A new account starts as Provider. The card confirms: the name followed by "is now" and the role, for example "Grace N. is now Triage."
  2. Then tap Approve. The button reads "Approving…" and the card confirms: the name followed by "is approved and can sign in now."
  3. The person taps Check again on their waiting screen and lands on their workspace.

Setting the role before approving means the person lands on the right workspace from their first sign-in. If you change someone's role later, ask them to sign out and sign in again: Settings, the Account card, Sign out, which needs a working connection.

What each role gets

Every account lands on its own workspace, the first tab in its list. The role also decides which sections of the visit form the person edits on the Board, Lab and Pharmacy screens and from New visit; these defaults can be changed per section in the template editor, which the admin guide covers.

RoleTabsEdits by default

An administrator gets Board, New visit, Analytics and Staff on top of their role's tabs, and edits every section of the visit form.

Make a second administrator

Do this before the first clinic day. With one administrator, a forgotten password means the dashboard route in section 15. On a trusted person's row, tap Make admin. The app asks first, with the name: "Make Grace N. an administrator?" and then "Admins approve and revoke accounts, and edit the organization's lists, formulary and form templates." Confirm, and the card says the name followed by "is now an administrator." The app will not let the last administrator remove themselves: on your own row, Revoke and Remove admin are disabled with the note "You are the only administrator. Make someone else an admin first."

When someone leaves

Revoke on an active row asks first: "Revoke access for" the name, then "They are locked out the next time their device talks to the server. Nothing they recorded is deleted, and you can restore them here later." Restore on a revoked row turns the account back on. The card's footer is the rule: "Revoking never deletes anything: visits an account recorded stay in the organization." The admin guide covers the Staff screen in full.

12. The board comes alive

Once an account is active on a device, two things run on their own. The device syncs every 20 seconds while it is online. And it listens for changes from the project, so a change made on another device arrives within seconds, without waiting for the next sync. The chip in the top bar tells you which state the device is in:

  • Live: "Connected live. Changes from other devices arrive within seconds." The chip says Live only while the device is actually listening and has nothing waiting.
  • Syncing: "Sending and receiving records..."
  • Synced: "All records are backed up to your cloud."
  • Connected: "Cloud connected. Not synced yet this session."
  • Offline: "Saved on this device. Will sync when you reconnect."
  • Sync error: "Tap to retry, or check your connection and credentials."

Tapping the chip runs a sync by hand. If anything is still waiting to upload, the app says so plainly rather than pretending: "Not backed up." followed by how many records are waiting and why.

Check it with two devices

  1. On a reception device, open the Board tab and tap + New patient. Fill the Visit and Patient sections for a test patient and tap Save. The visit appears as a card in the first column, Check-in.
  2. Watch your own device. Within seconds, the same card appears on your board, with the patient's name and No. followed by the patient number.
  3. On either device, tap Next: Triage on the card. It moves on both.
  4. On a device whose account is still waiting for approval, confirm it shows no records at all. Approve it, tap Check again on it, and confirm it now sees the test visit. Two minutes, and it exercises the whole access model.
  5. Delete the test visit in the app, not in the dashboard: open the Visits tab, open the patient's chart, and tap Delete on the visit. Deleting in the app marks the record deleted on every device; rows are never hard-deleted, by design.

13. Before the first clinic day

These are done under Settings while you are signed in as an administrator, on any device, once, and reach every device on its next sync. Each card says so in its own words; the Form templates card, for example: "These belong to your organization; a change reaches everyone on the next sync." For a non-administrator these editors are turned off with the note "Only an administrator account can change this. Ask your clinic's admin." The admin guide covers each editor in full; this is the short list.

  • Clinic lists. Sites and clinicians (you entered them in the wizard; check them), plus complaints, procedures and referral destinations. The same card holds Patient flow stations (clinic mode board): the columns of the board, one station per line, in board order. The shipped stations are Check-in, Triage, Provider, Lab, Pharmacy and Done. The card's help text is the rule to read before renaming any: "One station per line, in board order; the last station means done for the day. Station names are how visits point at a station, so renaming one strands today's visits under the old name. To rename mid-day, add the new name first and remove the old one after the day is done."
  • Lab tests. The tests your lab offers, each with a type and unit, and Ranges for numeric tests. The On by default box on each row is the one control for whether a test is on the visit form: untick it and tap Save lab tests to take a test off the form while keeping it in the panel; tick and save to put it back. The shipped panel has three tests off by default; the admin guide says how to offer one of those.
  • Formulary. The medications on the visit form. The card's warning: "saved prescriptions keep pointing at the same entry, so never reuse a row for a different drug - add a new row instead."
  • Diagnosis quick-picks and Prescription presets. One-tap buttons on the visit form. Hide what you do not use rather than deleting it.
  • Form templates. The visit form itself. Open the template editor; on each section row, Roles opens a grid of View and Edit checkboxes per role, with the defaults from the table in section 11 already ticked. The grid's own rule: "Edit includes View; a role with neither does not see the section. Administrators always see and edit every section."
  • A backup habit. On the Backup and restore card, Download backup saves a single file with every record on the device plus the settings. Take one at the end of each clinic day on at least one device. The cloud is a convenience copy for the devices; the backup file is the organization's own archive.

14. If the internet goes down

Clinic is built for a constant connection, and this guide will not pretend otherwise. What actually happens depends on how long the outage lasts.

A short outage

A device that is signed in keeps working on what it has. The chip reads Offline; visits saved on the device stay there and upload when the signal returns. What stops is the sharing: the board on each device stops receiving the others' changes, a lab result entered on one device does not reach the provider's device, and a visit registered at reception does not appear on the triage device until the connection is back. A device that has never signed in cannot start until it is online, because the account has to be confirmed once.

Do not sign anyone out during an outage. The Account card says why: "Records saved on this device stay here when you sign out - nothing is deleted. But signing back in needs a working connection, so do not sign out on a device that is offline mid-clinic." A device that stays disconnected for over a week shows a banner, "Reconnect to confirm your account.", and keeps working.

A long outage

The clinic keeps seeing patients on paper. Afterwards, the paper charts are typed up in DH EMR Field, the separate offline product, on any device with the Field app. The day's visits are then searchable, counted in Field's analytics and backed up in a file, instead of lost in a drawer. The Field admin guide explains that setup.

Said plainly: there is no way to move Field records into a Clinic organization today. Import from Field into Clinic is not available yet. Visits entered in Field stay in Field; they do not appear on the Clinic board, in Clinic search or in Clinic analytics. Keep the paper charts, because they are the source if the organization later needs those visits in Clinic. And do not point a Field device at the Clinic project: a Clinic project only accepts signed-in accounts, so a Field device's uploads are refused there.

15. If you get locked out

The schema has no self-service administrator path, on purpose, so these situations need the project dashboard's SQL Editor. Nothing else does. The Staff screen's footer points here: "If you ever lock yourself out entirely, the recovery steps live in the setup guide (SETUP.md), using the project dashboard."

The only administrator forgot their password

Email resets are unreliable without your own mail server, so create a replacement administrator instead. Authentication, then Users, then Add user, "Create new user" with a temporary password. Then, with the new email in place of NEW@EXAMPLE.ORG (one row must return):

UPDATE users_profiles SET is_admin = true, activated_at = now(), revoked_at = NULL
WHERE id = (SELECT id FROM auth.users WHERE lower(email) = lower('NEW@EXAMPLE.ORG'))
RETURNING id, is_admin, activated_at;

The organization has no administrator

The only administrator was revoked or deleted. Run the same statement against any existing trusted account, or a fresh one. The revoked_at = NULL part matters: promoting a once-revoked account without clearing the revocation does nothing, silently.

A device was revoked in the dashboard

The app can restore an account from the Staff screen, but it can never un-revoke a device. That is dashboard only:

UPDATE devices SET revoked_at = NULL WHERE id = '<device id>';

Keep the project awake

A free-tier project is paused after about a week with no traffic, and one paused long enough (around 90 days) can stop being restorable in place. A clinic that runs every day never hits this. If your organization runs seasonal missions, either upgrade the project to a paid tier, or put a calendar reminder to open the dashboard monthly and click Restore if prompted. And keep the device backups from section 13; they are the true archive.

16. Honest limits

  • Roles are a workflow layer, not a security wall. A role decides which workspace a person lands on and which sections of the visit form they edit by default. It does not hide records. Any approved account can read the organization's records, and the server cannot enforce permissions field by field. Approve the people you trust with the records, and revoke the ones you no longer do.
  • The organization's settings follow the account, not the device. Any device where an administrator is signed in can change the lists, formulary, lab tests, presets and forms for everyone, including a device that joined by link. Make admins sparingly.
  • The publishable key inside the join link is public by design. It unlocks nothing on its own, because every read and write needs an approved account. Anyone with the link can create an account that waits for your approval, and nothing more. Share the link with staff, not the public, and never confuse it with the secret key from section 5.
  • Revoking takes effect when the device next connects. A device that is offline keeps its last confirmed access until it talks to the server again. The Staff card says so, and after a week without a server check the device shows the reconnect banner.
  • A lost device keeps its data. Revoking an account or a device does nothing to the records already on it, and they are not encrypted at rest. Treat a lost device as a disclosure.
  • No password reset in the app. A forgotten password means a fresh account, or for the only administrator, the dashboard route in section 15.
  • Field cannot feed Clinic. Paper-day visits typed into DH EMR Field stay in Field. See section 14.
  • DH EMR is not a certified EHR and is not HIPAA-compliant. It must not be used where a certified EHR is required. It is intended for global-health use outside the US.
