DH EMR Clinic guides

Admin guide

This guide is for the person who runs DH EMR Clinic for an organization: the one who approves staff accounts, sets their roles, hands out the join link, keeps the forms, lists and formulary right, and looks after backups. For a staff member's day on the board, read the user guide. For standing up the organization's own cloud project, making the first admin and setting up your own device, read Setting up DH EMR Clinic for your organization.

Clinic has one kind of admin: the admin account, a staff account with the Admin flag. It gets the Staff tab, it unlocks the editors under Settings on whichever device it is signed in on, and the server trusts it to approve and revoke accounts and to change the organization's settings. Devices have no admin role in Clinic. Section 1 explains.

1. The admin account

Every staff account has a station role (Reception, Triage, Provider, Lab or Pharmacy) and an Admin flag. The Account card under Settings shows yours: a Name row, a Role row (for example "Provider"), and an Admin row reading "Yes" or "No". The flag is given from the Staff screen with Make admin (section 2). The very first admin of a new organization is made with one line in the project's SQL editor instead, because nobody is there yet to make them; the setup guide shows that line.

An admin account gets:

  • The Staff tab: approving, roles, revoking, the invite link.
  • The Board, Visits, New visit and Analytics tabs whatever the station role, and the role's own workspace first when the role is lab or pharmacy (the Lab or Pharmacy tab).
  • The whole visit form. The Roles grid says it in one line: "Administrators always see and edit every section."
  • The organization's settings editors under Settings, on whichever device the admin is signed in on: the clinic lists (sites, clinicians, complaints, procedures, referral destinations and the patient flow stations), the formulary, the lab test panel with its reference ranges, the diagnosis quick-picks and prescription presets, and the form templates with the Roles grid.
  • The server's trust. Approving, revoking, making admins, and writing the organization's settings to the cloud are accepted only from an admin account. A non-admin account that tries is refused by the server, not just by the screen.

What a non-admin sees under Settings

None of the editors are hidden. They are visible but turned off, and the reason is in the hover text of every disabled control: "Only an administrator account can change this. Ask your clinic's admin." The Clinic lists card says it in its own words: "Editing is turned off: only an administrator account can change clinic lists, the formulary or lab tests. Ask your clinic's admin." The Form templates card says: "The editor opens read-only: only an administrator account can change form templates. Ask your clinic's admin." The template editor and the reference range editor open with a "Read only" banner and the same reason. This is deliberate: anyone can see what exists and knows who to ask.

The gate is the account, never the device. A device that joined by the invite link is no different from the organization lead's own device: sign in as an admin on any device and the editors open; sign in as a non-admin on any device and they are off. There is no admin device to set up and nothing to change in the project's device list for it. The This device card under Settings shows the device's name, id and mode, and no role. If you also run DH EMR Field, note the difference: Field has no accounts, so there the gate is the device role chosen in its wizard.

Everything else in Settings works for any account: renaming the device, syncing, backup, restore and import.

2. The Staff screen: accounts

The Staff tab is shown to admin accounts only. It has two cards. Invite a device is first (section 3); Staff accounts is below it. The card explains itself: "Approve new accounts, set station roles, and revoke access. A new account can see nothing until it is approved here. Changes are enforced by the server: a device that is offline keeps its last confirmed access and is cut off the moment it reconnects."

Each account is one row: the person's name (or "Unnamed account"), "(you)" on your own row, a status chip reading "Pending", "Active" or "Revoked", a chip with the station role (for example "Triage"), and an "Admin" chip on admin accounts. The oldest account is first. Every change you make is confirmed in a message above the cards, and every refusal from the server appears there too, in the server's own words, after which the list reloads.

While nobody else has joined, the card says: "No one else is here yet. Connect a device with the invite link above; staff members then create their own accounts from its sign-in screen. New accounts appear on this list waiting for your approval, and they can see nothing until you approve them, so there is no rush."

Approving a new account

When a staff member creates an account from a device's sign-in screen, they land on a screen headed "Waiting for approval", and your Staff screen shows a note: "1 account is waiting for your approval." (or "3 accounts are waiting for your approval."). Set their station role first (next heading), then tap Approve on their row. The message reads, for example, "Grace N. is approved and can sign in now." On their device they tap Check again and the app opens on their role's workspace.

Until you approve, that account sees nothing at all: no visits, no board, no names. So there is no risk in an unknown account sitting on the list. Approve the people you know; leave the rest.

Setting the station role

Each row has a role list: Reception, Triage, Provider, Lab, Pharmacy. Pick one and the change saves at once; the message reads, for example, "Grace N. is now Triage." The role decides which tabs the person gets and which sections of the visit form they can fill in:

RoleTabsOn the visit form, by default
ReceptionBoard, Visits, New visit, SettingsFills in Visit and Patient; sees everything else read-only
TriageBoard, Visits, SettingsFills in Vitals, History, Chief Concern and Labs; sees Patient read-only
ProviderBoard, Visits, New visit, Analytics, SettingsFills in every section
LabLab, Visits, SettingsFills in Labs; sees Patient and Chief Concern read-only
PharmacyPharmacy, Visits, SettingsFills in Medications; sees Patient and Diagnosis read-only

Lab and pharmacy accounts have no Board tab; their workspace is the list of visits waiting on them. An admin gets every tab in the table plus Staff, with the Board placed right after their own role's workspace. What each role sees on the form is organization config, changed in the Roles grid (section 5). The user guide walks through each role's day.

If a row shows a role that is not on the list, for example "nurse" from an older version, the row shows the odd value as stored; the app treats "nurse" as Triage and any other unknown value as Provider. Pick a real role from the list and the odd value goes away.

A role change reaches the person's device the next time the app checks their account with the server. Closing and reopening the app does that at once. They land on the new role's workspace.

Revoking and restoring

Revoke appears on every active account. When you are the only admin, the one on your own row is turned off (the last-admin rule, below). The app confirms first:

The revoke confirm

"Revoke access for Grace N.?

They are locked out the next time their device talks to the server. Nothing they recorded is deleted, and you can restore them here later."

The message afterwards is honest about timing: "Grace N. is revoked. It takes effect when their device next connects." From that moment the server refuses every request from that account. On their device the next account check lands on a screen headed "This account has been turned off", which tells them: "The account for Grace N. was turned off by your organization's administrator. If you think that is a mistake, talk to them - they can turn it back on in a moment, and then Check again will let you straight in." and "No records have been lost. Anything this account already saved is safe with your organization."

A revoked account's row shows a "Revoked" chip and a Restore button. Tap it and the message reads "Grace N. is restored and can sign in again."; on their device Check again lets them in. The card's footer is the rule to remember: "Revoking never deletes anything: visits an account recorded stay in the organization."

Honest limit: a revoked device that is offline keeps working until it reconnects. The app keeps the last confirmed account on the device so a clinic does not stop when the wifi does, and nothing on the device can enforce a revocation it has not heard about. The moment the device reaches the server again, every request fails and the next check lands on the turned-off screen. If the device itself is lost, revoke the device too (section 4).

Making and removing admins

Make admin on a row confirms first:

The make-admin confirm

"Make Grace N. an administrator?

Admins approve and revoke accounts, and edit the organization's lists, formulary and form templates."

The message afterwards reads "Grace N. is now an administrator." and an "Admin" chip appears on the row. Remove admin on an admin's row takes the flag away without a confirm; the message reads "Grace N. is no longer an administrator." The person keeps their station role and their account; they lose the Staff tab and the extra tabs on their next account check.

The last-admin rule

An organization can never be left without an admin from inside the app. When you are the only active admin, your own Revoke and Remove admin buttons are turned off, and their hover text says why: "You are the only administrator. Make someone else an admin first." That is, word for word, the sentence the server itself raises if a request gets through anyway (for example two admins acting at the same moment); it then appears in the red banner at the top of the screen. Make a second admin before you change your own account.

Two other refusals can appear in that banner. "The server did not apply the change. Your account may not have permission to change this account." means the server quietly declined, most often because your own admin flag was removed on another device in the meantime. "You are signed out. Sign in and try again." means exactly that.

3. Inviting devices: the join link and QR code

The Invite a device card at the top of the Staff screen is how every device joins. Its own description: "Staff open this link on their device, or scan the code with its camera. The app sets itself up and shows sign-in; they create an account there, and you approve it below. Nobody types a project address or key."

  • Organization name: a text box with the hint "Shown on a device when it joins. It travels in the link, so set it before you share." Typing a name rebuilds the link and the code. The link keeps the first 80 characters of the name.
  • Join link: the link itself, in a read-only box that selects all when tapped. It starts with the app's address and carries everything the device needs after a #join= mark.
  • Copy link: copies it. The message reads "Link copied. Paste it into a message to your staff." If the browser refuses the clipboard: "Could not copy automatically. Select the link above and copy it by hand."
  • The QR code, drawn under the buttons. If a very long organization name makes the link too long for a code, the card says "The link is too long for a QR code. Share the link itself instead."

What happens on the staff member's device: opening the link (or scanning the code and opening the address the camera offers) opens DH EMR Clinic, which connects itself to your project, names itself after the device and the date, for example "iPad joined 2026-10-06", and shows the Sign in screen. A new staff member taps New here? Create an account, fills in Your name, Email and Password, and taps Create account. The sign-up screen tells them what to expect: "A new account starts with no access at all. Your administrator approves it from their device, and then you can sign in and work. That usually takes a moment, not a day." They appear on your Staff list as Pending. Someone who already has an account just signs in. The user guide covers the sign-in screens from the staff member's side.

The device's name can be changed later under Settings on the This device card with Rename this device; a rename is always safe (section 4). The organization's sites, clinicians and forms reach the device on its first sync after the account is approved.

If the link is opened on a device that is already connected to a different project, the app asks before switching:

The switch-project confirm

"This device is already connected to oldproject.supabase.co.

Switch it to Kabale Community Clinic (newproject.supabase.co)?

The device registers itself in the new project and signs in there. Visits already synced to the old project stay there; anything not yet backed up uploads to the new one."

Opened on a device already connected to the same project, the link does nothing. A damaged link says "That join link is damaged or incomplete. Ask your admin to send it again." A link made by a newer app than the device has says "That join link was made by a newer version of DH EMR Clinic. Update this app, then open the link again."

What the link carries, in the card's own words: "What the link carries: the project address, its publishable key and the organization name. The publishable key is public by design and unlocks nothing on its own, because every read and write needs an approved account. Anyone with the link can create an account that waits for your approval, so share it with staff, not the public."

A link cannot be expired from the app. It keeps working for as long as the project's publishable key does. That is fine, because a stranger with the link can only create an account you will never approve; keep approving only people you know.

4. Devices

A device registers itself in your project's device list the first time it works there. The This device card under Settings shows each device's Name, the first eight characters of its Device ID, and its Mode ("Cloud sync" on every Clinic device). A device has no admin role in Clinic (section 1); the role column you will see in the device list below is used by DH EMR Field only, and Clinic ignores it, so leave it alone.

Everything else about the fleet is done in the project dashboard's SQL Editor, on purpose, so that a stolen device cannot do any of it either. The queries are the ones from the setup guide, SETUP.md, which lives in the supabase folder of the DH EMR source code until it is published on this site.

List every device

Recognize every row. Match a row to a device by its name and by the first eight characters of the id shown on its This device card.

SELECT id, name, role, created_at, last_sync_at,
       CASE WHEN revoked_at IS NULL THEN 'active' ELSE 'REVOKED' END AS status
FROM devices ORDER BY last_sync_at DESC NULLS LAST;

Renaming is safe

Rename this device on the This device card opens a Device name box with Save name. The card says why it is safe: "Renaming keeps this device's identity: records it has already filed stay its own." A rename changes the label only and the app confirms, for example, "This device is now called "Reception iPad"." A blank name is refused: "A device name cannot be blank."

Revoking a lost or stolen device

Records the device already uploaded are kept. From the moment this runs, the project refuses records from that device:

UPDATE devices SET revoked_at = now() WHERE id = '<device id>';

Do two more things. On the Staff screen, Revoke the account that was signed in on the device, because the account is what reads the organization's records. And treat the loss as a disclosure: revoking does nothing to the records already on the device, and they are not encrypted at rest. The app can never un-revoke a device. If the device turns up again, the SQL editor is the only way back:

UPDATE devices SET revoked_at = NULL WHERE id = '<device id>';

Replacing a device

On a Clinic device the cloud already holds everything that synced, so a new device fills itself after it joins by the link and its account signs in. What a backup protects is anything that had not uploaded yet. Before wiping the old device, open its Cloud sync card under Settings: the Waiting to sync row must read "Nothing pending". If it does not, tap Sync now with a working connection; if the device cannot reach the server, tap Download backup on the Backup and restore card and restore that file on the new device with Restore from a backup. The Moving to a new device card under Settings lists the same four steps: back up the old device, connect the new one, restore the file, check the record count matches before wiping the old device.

If a device has both DH EMR Clinic and DH EMR Field installed, they keep separate records. Nothing crosses between them.

5. Form templates and the Roles grid

The visit form itself belongs to your organization: which forms are offered, their sections, your own questions, and which role sees which section. The Form templates card under Settings lists each form with "On" or "Off" next to it and has one button, Open the template editor. A new organization has one form, named "General Visit", already on; rename it if you like.

The editor is a panel headed Form templates. Every change is saved as you make it: the header reads "Saving…" and then "Saved". Its note applies to everything inside: "These belong to your organization. A change here reaches everyone on the next sync." Tap Close when you are done; there is nothing to discard. On a device that has not synced yet the editor refuses to open for writing: "Form settings have not reached this device yet", because "saving now would replace your organization's real form with an empty one. Sync this device, then come back." Do not work around that guard.

Forms

Each form has an editable name box, a checkbox captioned On, and the buttons Sections, Duplicate and Delete. The card says what "On" means: "Turn a form on to offer it for new visits; with more than one on, the visit form shows a selector. Every visit keeps the name of the form it was filed under." To add a form, type a name in New form name and tap Add form. Duplicating first is the safe way to experiment with a working form; the copy is named after the original with "(copy)". The editor never strands the team: it refuses to delete the last form ("At least one form must remain. Add another form before deleting this one.") and refuses to turn off the last form that is on ("At least one form must stay on, or there is no visit form to open. Turn another form on first."). Deleting a form confirms with "Visits already filed under it keep their answers and their template name. The form just stops being offered for new visits."

Sections

Tap Sections on a form. The card is headed "Sections of" and the form's name, and explains: "Reorder with the arrows, hide sections you do not use, rename them, and add your own sections with questions. The lists the built-in sections offer, like medications and lab tests, are edited on the Settings screen."

Each section is one row with ↑ and ↓ arrows, an editable title box, a chip reading "Built in" or the number of questions, a Roles button, and a Hide or Show button. A renamed built-in section gets Reset name. Visit and Patient show a disabled Always shown button, because every visit needs them. Add a section adds your own section at the end; custom sections also get Questions, Delete, and a checkbox Starts collapsed on the visit form. Deleting a custom section confirms with "Answers already saved on existing records are kept, but will no longer appear on the form."

Under Questions, each question has arrows, a label box, a type list (Short text, Long text, Single choice, Multiple choice, Number, Scale (0-N), Yes / No, Date), a Required checkbox and Remove. Choice questions get Choices ("Choices, one per line"); scale questions get Scale with Lowest and Highest. Add one with New question and Add question. Every question shows its permanent field id, so removing a question never touches saved answers.

The Roles grid: who sees which section

This is the part of the editor that makes Clinic a clinic. Tap Roles on any section row (it becomes Done with roles) and a small grid opens under the row. Its note: "Who sees the Vitals section on the visit form. Edit includes View; a role with neither does not see the section. Administrators always see and edit every section." While the section is still on the shipped defaults the note ends with "These are the built-in defaults."

The grid has three columns, Role, View and Edit, and five rows: Reception, Triage, Provider, Lab, Pharmacy. Each cell is a checkbox. Ticking Edit ticks View as well; clearing View clears Edit. A role with neither box ticked does not see the section at all. Every tick saves at once, the row gains a "Roles changed" chip, and a Reset to default button appears; tapping it removes your change and the section goes back to the shipped defaults. Like everything in the editor, the grid is turned off for a non-admin account.

The shipped defaults, section by section. "Edit" means the role fills it in; "View" means read-only; a blank means the role does not see it:

How the form behaves for a role, so you can predict a change before you make it:

  • A view-only section shows a "View only" chip in its header and cannot be typed into. A hidden section is simply not there. The form's subtitle names the slice, for example "Triage sections".
  • On a new visit (from New visit or + New patient), Visit and Patient are editable for every role, whatever the grid says, and view-only sections are left out. That is why reception's registration is a short intake form.
  • A save writes only the sections the role could edit. Every other section is kept from the stored visit, so a pharmacy save never blanks the vitals that triage entered on another device.
  • The role's slice applies everywhere the form opens: on the Board, on the Lab and Pharmacy screens, from New visit, and from the Visits tab, where Edit and + New visit on a patient's chart open the role's view too. The chart itself still shows every section of a visit, read-only, to every role.

Example. Your triage nurses also record the working diagnosis before the provider sees the patient. Open Roles on the Diagnosis section and tick Edit for Triage. From the next sync, triage sees and fills in Diagnosis on the board; the provider still edits everything. Change your mind with Reset to default.

Honest limit: roles are a workflow layer, not a security wall. The grid decides what a role's form shows and saves. It does not stop anyone from reading records. Any approved account can read the organization's records, for example from a patient's chart on the Visits tab, and the server cannot enforce field-level permissions. Approve only people you trust with the whole chart.

6. Clinic lists and patient flow stations

The Clinic lists card under Settings explains itself: "These fill the pick lists on the visit form: sites, clinicians, complaints, procedures and referral destinations. On a cloud device your organization's lists replace these on the next sync. One entry per line, and commas belong inside an entry - "Grace N., clinical officer" is one clinician." Six boxes, each with its own save button:

  • Clinic or site names, saved with Save sites
  • Clinicians, saved with Save clinicians
  • Complaints, saved with Save complaints: the buttons under the Chief concern box
  • Procedures, saved with Save procedures
  • Referral destinations, saved with Save referral destinations
  • Patient flow stations (clinic mode board), saved with Save stations: the columns of the Board

The format is the same everywhere: one entry per line, and commas belong inside an entry. Blank lines are dropped and duplicates are removed. After a save the app confirms, for example "Saved 3 sites. Reopen the visit form to see them." Sites and clinicians can never be saved empty, because every visit needs a site and the form could never save; the app refuses with "You need at least one site. An empty list would block every save." The other lists refuse too: "This list cannot be saved empty. One complaint per line."

Until real sites and clinicians are set, every visit is filed under the placeholders "Site A" and "Physician A", and Settings shows a red warning above the card: "This device is still filing records under placeholder names." Fix this before the first clinic day. Placeholder names are no use in a report.

Patient flow stations

The stations are the columns of the Board, in order. The shipped list is Check-in, Triage, Provider, Lab, Pharmacy, Done. The box's own help text is the rule that matters:

The stations box, in its own words

"One station per line, in board order; the last station means done for the day. Station names are how visits point at a station, so renaming one strands today's visits under the old name. To rename mid-day, add the new name first and remove the old one after the day is done."

So: the last line is the done-for-the-day column, which the Board keeps collapsed. A visit points at its station by name. If you rename "Provider" to "Doctor" at noon, every visit sitting under Provider is no longer on any column; the Board does not lose them, but they drop off the columns until someone moves them. Add the new name first, move the visits, and remove the old name after the day is done. After a save the app confirms with "Saved 6 stations. The board picks them up on its next refresh."

Two more things depend on names. A signed-in role's own column carries a "your station" tag: reception's is always the first station, whatever it is called; triage, provider, lab and pharmacy find a station spelled like their role (capitals do not matter). Rename "Triage" to "Nursing" and triage staff lose the tag, nothing else. The shipped Lab column, between Provider and Pharmacy, is where the other roles park a patient who is at the lab: the Lab team itself works from the Lab tab, which lists visits by ordered tests, not by column, and lab accounts have no Board tab unless they are admins. If your flow never sends a patient to the lab and back, remove the Lab station; the Lab tab works the same without it.

7. Formulary

The Formulary card under Settings holds the medications offered on the visit form: a table with the columns Medication, Dose, Unit, Category and Controlled, a Remove button on each row, and the buttons Add medication and Save formulary. Clinic ships with 38 entries, from "Amoxicillin 500mg" to "Normal Saline 500ml". The formulary cannot be saved empty; after a save the app confirms, for example, "Saved 38 medications. Reopen the visit form to see them."

One rule matters more than the rest. The card's own words: "Edit carefully: saved prescriptions keep pointing at the same entry, so never reuse a row for a different drug - add a new row instead." Editing a row to become a different drug silently changes what old prescriptions appear to say, and what the pharmacy screen shows for them. Add new rows; remove rows you no longer stock.

The dose in the formulary fills the dose box on the visit form when the drug is picked. The unit decides whether the app can work out a quantity for the pharmacy: it counts "tabs", "caps" and "sachet". A line with another unit, for example "ml" or "cream", records no quantity, and on the Pharmacy screen that line reads "no quantity worked out, count by hand". The pharmacy's Qty box is prefilled from the computed quantity, so a formulary with the right units saves them typing on every line.

8. Lab tests and reference ranges

The Lab tests card defines the lab panel on the visit form: a table with the columns Test, Type, Unit, On by default and Ranges, a Remove button on each row, and the buttons Add lab test and Save lab tests. A test's type is "Positive / negative" (POS and NEG buttons on the form) or "Number" (a number box with the unit as its hint). The card says: "Saving replaces the whole panel for your organization." The list cannot be saved empty.

In Clinic the lab panel also drives the Lab workspace. On the Labs section of a visit, staff tick Ordered next to a test; a visit with a test ordered and no result yet appears on the lab role's Lab tab, longest-waiting first, and leaves it when the result is entered. Removing a test from the panel removes it from the form, but results already saved on visits are kept.

On by default

The On by default checkbox is the one control for whether a test is offered on the visit form; there is no separate list of hidden tests to manage. Ticked, the test is on the form. Unticked, the test is hidden on the visit form: it stays in your panel, so the list is not cluttered with tests the clinic does not run, and results already saved for it are not touched. Untick and tap Save lab tests to take a test off the form; tick and save to put it back. Like the rest of the panel, the change reaches every device on the next sync.

Three built-in tests ship unticked: "Hepatitis C (Anti-HCV)", "COVID-19 Rapid" and "TB (AFB Smear)". If your clinic runs one of them, tick its box and save, and it is on the visit form like any other test.

Reference ranges: new results only

On a Number test, the Ranges button opens the reference range editor, headed Reference ranges. On a row that has not been saved yet the button is off, with the reason "Save lab tests first, then set the reference ranges for this test." The card explains what ranges do: "On a numeric test, Ranges sets the bands that interpret a value as it is entered; changing them affects new results only, and visits already saved keep the interpretation recorded at the time." The editor repeats that rule in a note, and it governs everything here:

The editor's standing notice

"Changing ranges affects new results only.

Visits already saved keep the interpretation that was recorded at the time; editing ranges never rewrites a recorded result. Only new results entered after this change use the new ranges."

Each range is one row: a Label (for example "Normal"), From (at least), Up to (below), and a Color: None, Green (normal), Amber (caution), Red (critical) or Gray (neutral). An empty bound is open. Add rows with Add range, reorder with the arrows, remove with Remove. The matching rule: "Ranges are checked top to bottom and the first match wins. A value equal to "from" is inside the range; a value equal to "up to" is not - it belongs to the next range up."

Before you save, a box headed "Check these before saving (saving is still allowed):" lists anything odd: a range with no label, a range that can never match, two ranges that overlap, a gap no range covers, or a bound that is not a number. Read it. Use Try a value at the bottom to see exactly what a new result would record, for example "Would record 182 mg/dL with interpretation Elevated". Then tap Save ranges; Cancel asks "Discard the range changes?" if you edited anything. The default bands: Blood Glucose in mg/dL is Low below 70, Normal from 70, Elevated from 140 and High from 200. Hemoglobin in g/dL is Severe Anemia below 7, Moderate Anemia from 7, Mild Anemia from 10 and Normal from 12.

9. Diagnosis quick-picks and prescription presets

Both live in their own cards under Settings, and both belong to the organization: "a change reaches everyone on the next sync." Diagnosis quick-picks are the buttons under the Diagnosis box on the visit form; each tap appends to the diagnosis text. Prescription presets are one-tap bundles in the Medications section; applying one adds its medication lines and its treatment notes to the visit, and only ever adds.

In both cards each entry is one row with ↑ and ↓, a Hide or Show button, and Remove. Hide keeps the entry in your list but takes its button off the visit form; the quick-picks card's hover text says it plainly: "Hidden entries stay in the list but do not appear on the visit form." Show reverses it. Remove deletes the entry from the list; saved visits are never touched, and the confirm says so: "Diagnoses already saved on visits keep their text - only the quick-pick button goes away." and, for a preset, "Prescriptions already saved on visits are kept, and the formulary is not changed - only the one-tap preset goes away." Removing a built-in adds one more line: "This is a built-in preset, so once saved it stays removed for your organization after sync." Nothing is written until you tap Save diagnosis quick-picks or Save prescription presets, and neither list can be saved empty; the quick-picks card refuses with "Hide entries instead if you do not want them on the visit form." and the presets card says the same of presets.

Add a quick-pick by typing it in the box with the hint "Add a diagnosis" and tapping Add diagnosis. Build a preset with Add preset (or Edit on an existing one): an editor opens with a Preset name box, medication lines picked from the formulary (or "Other drug (type a name)"), a dose, a frequency and a duration per line, + Add medication, and a Treatment notes added with this preset (optional) box. Finish with Add this preset or Apply changes. Under each line the editor says what the preset will record, for example "14 tabs", or "Not in the formulary - prescriptions from this line record no dispensing quantity." Check those lines before saving: they are what the pharmacy screen will show.

Clinic ships with 31 quick-picks, from "Malaria" to "Abscess", and 21 presets, from "H. pylori Triple Therapy" to "MSK Pain".

10. Backup and restore

In Clinic the cloud is the live copy, and every device that syncs holds a copy of the organization's records as far as it has synced. The backup file is still the organization's archive: a cloud project can be paused, deleted, or outlive its free tier, and the file is the copy that is yours. The Backup and restore card under Settings says what a backup is: "A backup is a single file holding every record on this device plus your form and preset settings. It never contains your cloud key or device identity. Take one at the end of each clinic day - it is the safety net if a device is lost, wiped, or replaced."

Make it a habit on one device that has synced: one tap on Download backup at the end of each clinic day, and the file moved somewhere safe. The app confirms with the count: "Backup downloaded with 140 records. Keep it somewhere safe." If a device ever shows the warning "Back this device up today." above the screen, do it then, on that device.

Restore merges; it never wipes

Tap Restore from a backup and pick the file. The card's own footer is the rule: "Restoring merges: nothing already on this device is lost, and where the same record exists in both the more recently saved version wins." A restore cannot undo newer work. The app confirms first:

The records restore confirm

"Restore records from "dh-emr-backup-2026-10-06.json"?

Records in the file are merged into this device. Where the same record exists in both, the more recently saved version is kept, so this cannot undo newer work."

Then a second question, about settings:

The settings restore confirm

"Also restore the settings from this file?

That replaces this device's form layout, sites, providers and formulary with the ones in the backup. If you are an administrator account those settings go out to the whole team on the next sync.

Choose Cancel to restore records only. That is almost always what you want."

In Clinic, read "admin device" in that question as "a device where an administrator is signed in": that is when restored settings go out to the organization. Take the last line seriously there: restoring months-old settings and syncing would roll the whole organization's forms, lists and formulary back. Tap Cancel unless you are deliberately restoring configuration. The result message tells you exactly what happened, for example "Restored: 12 added, 3 updated, 125 already newer here. 140 records on this device. Settings were left as they are. Restored records will upload on the next sync." Restored records upload to the cloud like any other, so a restore on one device reaches every device.

The separate Moving from another cloud project card is for one job: bringing records across from a project this device is not connected to, for example after moving to a new Supabase project. Export the records from the old project as JSON, then tap Import records from a file. The card's warning: "Export as JSON, not CSV. CSV flattens lab results and medication lists into text and they cannot be read back reliably."

11. Recovery: locked out, no admin left, forgotten passwords

The Staff screen's footer points here: "If you ever lock yourself out entirely, the recovery steps live in the setup guide (SETUP.md), using the project dashboard." The schema has no self-service admin path on purpose, so exactly these situations need the dashboard, and nothing else does.

A staff member forgot their password

There is no password reset in the app. The sign-in screen says why: "Forgot your password? Your administrator can set up a fresh account for you - password reset emails are not reliable on most clinic projects." Two ways to give them a fresh account:

  • They tap New here? Create an account on the sign-in screen and create a new account with a different email address. It appears on your Staff list as Pending; set the role and approve it.
  • Or you create it for them in the dashboard: Authentication, then Users, then Add user, choose "Create new user" and set a temporary password. Do not use "Send invitation"; it depends on the same unreliable mailer. The account appears on your Staff list as Pending, named after the part of the email before the @ sign; set the role and approve it.

Then Revoke the old account. Nothing it recorded is lost; visits stay with the organization. The old account can be restored later if the password turns up.

The only admin forgot their password, or the organization has no admin left

Create a replacement admin, or promote a trusted existing account, from the dashboard. First, if a new account is needed: Authentication, then Users, then Add user, "Create new user", with a temporary password. Then, in the SQL Editor, one line; it must return exactly one row (zero rows means the email did not match or the account does not exist yet):

UPDATE users_profiles SET is_admin = true, activated_at = now(), revoked_at = NULL
WHERE id = (SELECT id FROM auth.users WHERE lower(email) = lower('NEW@EXAMPLE.ORG'))
RETURNING id, is_admin, activated_at;

The revoked_at = NULL part matters: promoting a once-revoked account without clearing the revocation does nothing, silently. Sign in with that account on a Clinic device; it opens on the Board with the Staff tab. This is the same line the setup guide uses for the very first admin, and its recovery section repeats these steps.

A revoked device

Accounts are restored from the Staff screen (section 2). Devices are not: the app can never un-revoke a device, and the SQL editor line in section 4 is the only way back.

Where the SQL and the setup guide live

The setup guide, SETUP.md, and the two SQL files that set up and check a project, setup.sql and verify.sql, live in the supabase folder of the DH EMR source code until they are published on this site. The setup guide also has a row-count check to run before and after any change to the project, and a note on free-tier pausing: a project that goes a week without traffic is paused, and one paused long enough can stop being restorable in place. A clinic that runs all day is never idle that long; a seasonal one is. Keep the backup files either way.

12. The demo

The Clinic demo is a fictional clinic day already in progress: patients waiting at check-in, in triage, with the provider, at the lab and at the pharmacy, and simulated colleagues who keep working while you watch. A demo stays in the browser and never syncs anywhere. It refuses every cloud connection on purpose, so nothing done in a demo can touch a real organization, and a demo cannot be used to run a clinic.

A side panel headed You are simulating is where you pick the role you are trying: Reception, Triage, Provider, Lab, Pharmacy or Admin, each landing on its own workspace. Pick Admin to rehearse this guide: the Staff screen over a fictional roster (approve, set roles, revoke, make admin), and every Settings editor as the real thing: clinic lists and stations, formulary, lab tests and ranges, quick-picks and presets, and the template editor with the Roles grid. Then switch to Triage or Pharmacy in the panel and open a visit to see what your Roles grid change did. Build a preset, set a reference range, add a section, break things freely; Reset demo in the banner puts the clinic day back. The panel's Act now makes a colleague act at once, and Pause activity stops them while you look at something.

What the demo cannot show is anything that needs a real backend: the sync chip, Cloud sync, and the Invite a device card are turned off there, and sign-in and sign-out are simulated through the panel. The Field demo is the separate product's demo, with the same Settings editors and no accounts.

13. Honest limits

  • Roles are a workflow layer, not a security wall. The Roles grid decides what each role's form shows and saves. Any approved account can read the organization's records, and the server cannot enforce field-level permissions. Approve only people you trust with the whole chart.
  • The publishable key in the join link is public by design. It unlocks nothing on its own, because every read and write needs an approved account. A link cannot be expired from the app; it works for as long as the project's key does. Share it with staff, not the public, and approve only people you know.
  • Revocation waits for the device to reconnect. A device that is offline keeps its last confirmed account and is cut off the moment it reconnects. After a week without reaching the server, the app shows the banner "Reconnect to confirm your account." but keeps working.
  • A lost device keeps its data. Every device that syncs holds a local copy of the organization's records as far as it has synced. Revoking the device and its account stops uploads and sign-ins; it does nothing to what is already on the device, and that data is not encrypted at rest.
  • Settings follow the account, not the device. Any device where an admin account is signed in, including one that joined by link, can change the organization's lists, formulary, lab tests, presets and forms for everyone. A non-admin sees the editors turned off with the note "Only an administrator account can change this. Ask your clinic's admin." Make admins sparingly, and revoke an admin's account, not just their device, when they leave.
  • Clinic needs the internet. A device keeps working on what it has while the connection is down and syncs when it returns, but nothing new from other devices arrives in the meantime. For days when the backend is down, DH EMR Field is the paper-chart entry app, and there is no way to move Field records into a Clinic organization today.
  • DH EMR is not a certified EHR and is not HIPAA-compliant. It must not be used where a certified EHR is required. It is intended for global-health use outside the US.
